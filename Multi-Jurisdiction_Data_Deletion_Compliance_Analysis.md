# Multi-Jurisdiction Compliance Challenge
* **Prepared By:** Fred Pekyi
* ## Overview

This document provides a detailed legal and compliance analysis for ShopGhana, a Ghanaian e-commerce platform operating internationally. The analysis addresses three simultaneous data deletion requests submitted by customers located in Ghana, Germany, and the United States (California). Each request is evaluated under the applicable data protection framework, with clear obligations, timelines, exemptions, and drafted responses. The goal is to demonstrate correct interpretation of multi-jurisdictional privacy laws and practical compliance implementation.

---

## Customer A: Abena (Accra, Ghana)

**Applicable Law:** Ghana Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843)

### Legal Right to Deletion

Under the Ghana Data Protection Act, data subjects have the right to request the deletion, correction, or blocking of personal data. This right exists but is conditional. It must be balanced against the data controller’s legal and legitimate business obligations.

### ShopGhana’s Obligations

ShopGhana is required to verify Abena’s identity, assess whether continued data retention is legally justified, delete personal data that is no longer necessary, and ensure that any retained data is securely stored and access-restricted. The company must respond within a reasonable timeframe.

### Data Retention and Justification

ShopGhana may lawfully retain limited data such as financial transaction records, invoices, and audit logs where required by tax, accounting, or commercial laws in Ghana. These records are typically retained for five to seven years and must not be used for marketing or profiling purposes.

### Response Deadline

The Ghana Data Protection Act does not specify a fixed response deadline. However, industry best practice requires responding within approximately 21 to 30 days.

### Draft Response to Abena

Dear Abena,
Your request to delete your ShopGhana account has been processed. Your personal profile and marketing-related information have been removed from our systems. Certain transaction records have been securely retained where required by Ghanaian law for accounting and audit purposes. These records are access-restricted and will be deleted once the legally required retention period expires.

## Customer B: Lukas (Berlin, Germany)

**Applicable Law:** EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)

### Legal Right to Erasure

Lukas has a clear right to erasure under Article 17 of the GDPR. This right applies when personal data is no longer necessary for the purposes for which it was collected. However, the right is not absolute and is subject to legal exemptions.

### Applicable Exemptions

ShopGhana may retain certain data where processing is necessary for compliance with legal obligations, such as tax and accounting requirements, or for the establishment, exercise, or defense of legal claims. Because Lukas completed a purchase two weeks ago, transaction and VAT records must be retained.

### Required Action Steps

ShopGhana must verify Lukas’s identity, delete or anonymize his account profile, marketing data, and analytics records, restrict access to legally required financial records, notify relevant third-party processors of the erasure request, and document the compliance action.

### Response Deadline

ShopGhana must respond within one month (30 days). This period may be extended by up to two additional months if the request is complex, provided the customer is informed.

### Consequences of Missing the Deadline

Failure to comply may result in complaints to a supervisory authority, significant administrative fines of up to €20 million or 4% of global annual turnover, and reputational damage.

### Draft Response to Lukas

Dear Lukas,
We have completed your request under Article 17 of the GDPR. Your personal account data has been erased, including marketing and analytics information. Certain transaction records have been retained solely to meet legal and tax obligations and are access-restricted. You may contact your local Data Protection Authority if you require further assistance.

## Customer C: Maria (Los Angeles, California)

**Applicable Law:** California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) / California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA)

### Legal Rights Under CCPA/CPRA

Maria has the right to request deletion of personal information, to opt out of the sale or sharing of personal data, and to be informed about the categories of data collected, retained, and shared.

### Deletion Request Assessment

ShopGhana cannot fully process Maria’s deletion request immediately because there is an active return dispute. CCPA and CPRA allow businesses to retain personal data when necessary to complete transactions, resolve disputes, or prevent fraud.

### Opt-Out of Sale or Sharing

ShopGhana must immediately honor Maria’s request to stop selling or sharing her personal data. This includes disabling data sharing with advertisers or partners and recording her preference in a Do Not Sell or Share registry.

### Response Deadline

ShopGhana must respond within 45 days. A single extension of an additional 45 days is permitted if the customer is notified.

### Required Disclosures

ShopGhana must inform Maria about the categories of personal data retained, the purpose for retention, the third parties with whom data was previously shared, and confirmation that the opt-out request has been honored.

### Draft Response to Maria

Dear Maria,
We have honored your request to opt out of the sale or sharing of your personal information, effective immediately. Your deletion request is currently limited due to an active return dispute, as permitted under California law. Once the dispute is resolved, we will complete the deletion of eligible personal data and notify you accordingly.

## Practical Implementation Challenges

Operating across multiple jurisdictions introduces challenges such as differing response timelines, conflicting retention requirements, coordination with third-party processors, handling active disputes while respecting deletion rights, and balancing compliance obligations with fraud prevention and financial recordkeeping. Effective governance, clear internal procedures, and jurisdiction-aware compliance workflows are essential to meeting these challenges.

---

## Conclusion

This analysis demonstrates how ShopGhana can lawfully and responsibly handle data deletion requests across Ghanaian, European, and Californian legal frameworks. By applying jurisdiction-specific rules, recognizing lawful exemptions, and providing transparent communication to customers, the company ensures regulatory compliance while maintaining operational integrity.
